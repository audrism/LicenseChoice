\section{Introduction}\label{intro}

Open Source Software (OSS) is a cornerstone of modern software
development, providing a collaborative environment that fosters
innovation, knowledge exchange, and the widespread use of software
tools and libraries. 
The licensing of OSS projects is crucial as it dictates how software
can be used, modified, and distributed. As the number of OSS
projects has surged, understanding the intricacies of license
selection has become increasingly complex. 

Existing work either lacks scale or comprehensive identificaion of
licenses to fully reflect license use in OSS. More importantly,
licence choice, like the choice of
technology~\cite{choice23,ma2020methodology}, may not only reflect strategic
decisions by project maintainers to balance openness, protection,
and control over their codebases, or be influenced by matters of
technical convenience such as licenses that are used by frameworks
they rely on, social convenience such as licennses used by their
collaborators, or, simply by awareness (i.i.e., license
popularity). **work backwards from the regresssion predictors to 
see how they fit within the choice literature and within prior
license work literature. For example, language and calendar time may be a proxy
for GPL popularity with older products and C more exposed to  gpl***.

Prior work only tabulated observed license use, but have not
attempted to *check* to observe evolution of license type use over
time, nor to model explicitly how the context, such as project size,
programming language and other aspects of the project (or individual
developer) influence license choice. 

To adress these gaps, we aim to create a model that
a) accounts for all types, minor variations, and non-semantic
variations comprehensively, b) is applied to a sample that
aproximates entire OSS-scale, c) includes key context predictors that are
likely to affect differing priorities in license choce among
projects and individuals.

The core questions our research seeks to answer are:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{RQ1}: What are the types, prevalence, and evolution of licenses across the entire open source software landscape?
    \item \textbf{RQ2}: How the characteristics of a project influence the choice of its license?   
\end{itemize}
