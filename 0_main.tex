\begin{abstract}
Un- or incorrect-licensed Open Source Software (OSS) code can
poison supply chains and inhibit innovation. Many OSS developers are not aware of these implications or make choices that are
suboptimal. We aim to understand the current state of OSS licensing by developing a preliminary theory and model for license choice and testing it using a comprehensive and nearly complete dataset of OSS licenses. Our theory makes four sets of predictions
on license ``stickiness'' and choice based on a number of observable project context factors. In contrast to previous work, we find that more than 80\% of publicly available projects do not have a license. We find that copyleft licenses are most sticky, permissive licenses most common, that social, technical, and ideological factors affect the choice of license. 
Future research into better understanding
reasons for and ways to change the ``no license'' defaults and devise
ways to anticipate license type for a project would have the most
practical impact on reducing licensing risks and enable more
innovation.
\end{abstract}
