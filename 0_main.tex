\begin{abstract}
  The terms of how publicly available source code can be used are
  dictated by its license. The license (or its absence), in turn,
  affects what code the project may reuse and how its code can be
  (re)used and may also affect external participation and overall
  activity of the project. We aim to better understand the general
  state of license distribution overall and within language
  ecosystems and to investigate if license changes are associated
  with a noticeable variations of project output. To accomplish that
  we identify licenses and license types for over 100M software
  projects and find that most do not contain any license, that
  permissive licenses represent the bulk of most licenses, and that
  permissive licensing is representing an increasing proportion of
  all licenses over time. Restrictive licenses are more likely to
  be retained, however. There is a great variation among language
  ecosystems with C-language strongly favoring restrictive
  licenses. We then study the subset of projects (23,619, or roughly
  2.4\% of licensed projects) that underwent a license-type change,
  comparing activity within one year of the adoption of the initial
  and final licenses. This analysis
  shows that the change from restrictive to permissive license varies
  with the ecosystem. C-language ecosystems show reduced activity
  while Python shows increased activity when comparing restrictive to
  permissive license transition. Our results demonstrate dramatic
  changes in license type prevalence over time and find that the
  effects of license changes may have opposite effects depending on
  the language ecosystem.  
\end{abstract}

\section{Data Availability}
The original replication package, including the cP2all.1y/2y datasets and the
R notebook, is available at \url{https://zenodo.org/records/15031139}.
Additional code and intermediate data for the robustness analyses in
Section~\ref{sec:robustness}---including the pre-switch window aggregates
(\texttt{cP2pre\_post.1y}), the popularity-augmented R script, and the
year-before/year-after analysis script---are included in the supplementary
materials submitted alongside this manuscript and will be added to a new
