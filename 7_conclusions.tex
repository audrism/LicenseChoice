\section{Conclusions}

Two findings emerge clearly.
\textbf{(1)} 83\% of OSS projects lack a formal license, four times the
rate reported by prior studies that filtered on stars or
package-manager presence; this exposes a much larger long tail of
legally-ambiguous reusable code than was previously known.
\textbf{(2)} For projects that switch license types, the apparent
global effects of switching to a permissive license on files,
upstream and downstream project counts collapse under
popularity controls and are not robust. What remains robust across
designs is a language-ecosystem-mediated pattern: C/C++ projects show
reduced activity after switching to permissive licenses, Python the
opposite. The V2604-aligned, bot-filtered year-before/year-after
reanalysis surfaces small ($\sim 4\%$) but consistent attenuations in
commits, distinct human authors, and sustained activity under
restrictive$\rightarrow$permissive transitions, suggesting future work
should combine the regression with qualitative case studies in
specific language communities.
